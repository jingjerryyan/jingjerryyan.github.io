My Chinese Name: 闫璟 (Yán Jǐng)

This page offers a brief introduction to my name and story. I go by Jing (Jerry) in Hong Kong and the U.S., though I was born and raised in Hohhot, Inner Mongolia. My Chinese name often poses pronunciation challenges for non-Mandarin speakers, and this guide explains its meaning.

闫 Yán surname

Pronounced yán with a rising tone, like “yen.” The character began as the word for the gate of a village lane — you can still see the 门 (gate) framing it — and today it lives on almost only as a family name, written 閆 in traditional script.

璟 Jǐng given name

Pronounced jǐng with a dipping tone — the voice falls, then rises. 璟 means the luster of jade: the jade radical 王 sits beside 景, “bright light,” which also gives the character its sound. It is a literary character, chosen for names to wish someone a quiet, lasting brilliance.
